Concrete Driveway Installation and Repair in Shreveport, LA

Why Driveways Fail in Louisiana

Concrete fails when the prep work is wrong, the reinforcement is missing, or the curing is rushed. In Northwest Louisiana, those mistakes get punished fast. Our expansive clay soil swells when wet and contracts when dry, putting constant stress on any slab above it. A slab poured without proper base preparation or steel reinforcement will crack within a season or two. We see it regularly on properties where a previous contractor cut corners before the truck ever showed up.

Repair or Replace? The Honest Answer

Small cracks and surface damage can often be repaired to extend your driveway's life by years. If your driveway has large cracks, multiple sunken areas, or widespread crumbling, replacement makes more sense. We assess your situation and recommend the most cost-effective solution.

Common Driveway Problems We Fix

Cracks and Surface Damage

Cracks form when the base beneath your driveway shifts or when water works its way into small openings and expands. We clean out damaged areas, fill cracks with professional-grade materials, and seal the surface to prevent further water intrusion. For concrete driveways, we can patch sections or resurface the entire slab depending on how far the damage has spread.

Sunken or Settled Areas

When the soil beneath your driveway compacts or washes away, sections of concrete sink below the original level. This creates trip hazards and allows water to pool on the surface. We lift sunken sections back to grade or remove and replace them with proper base preparation. A stable base prevents the same problem from coming back.

Drainage Issues

Water that sits on your driveway or flows toward your foundation causes serious damage over time. We evaluate how water moves across your property and make adjustments to redirect it away from problem areas. Sometimes a simple grade adjustment fixes the issue. Other times we need to add drainage solutions or rebuild sections with the correct slope.

How We Build New Driveways

Site Prep and Base Compaction

Good concrete starts below the surface. We excavate to the correct depth, remove weak or unstable soil where needed, and compact a proper base before forming. This step is invisible once the job is done, but it determines whether your slab stays flat in five years or starts to heave and crack.

Thickness for the Load

A residential walkway and a driveway carrying trucks do not need the same thickness. We size each slab to what it will carry. We do not apply one spec to every job.

SteelX Reinforcement Included as Standard

Every slab we pour includes our SteelX reinforcement system. Not an upgrade. Not an add-on. Reinforcement controls cracking, adds tensile strength, and holds the slab together if the soil shifts beneath it. We do not pour slabs without it.

Control Joints in the Right Places

Concrete will crack. The goal is to control where. We cut control joints at planned intervals based on slab size and shape, a basic step that a surprising number of crews skip or space wrong.

Proper Curing

Concrete that dries too fast loses strength and surfaces early. We use curing compounds and cover the slab when conditions call for it. You will not use the slab until it is ready.

Finish Options

  • Broom finish: the most common residential choice, clean, consistent, good traction
  • Smooth trowel finish: flat and polished, suited for covered patios and shop floors
  • Exposed aggregate: the stone in the mix is revealed at the surface for a textured, decorative look
  • Stamped concrete: can mimic brick, stone, or tile for a higher-end appearance

We will recommend the right option for your project before work begins.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a concrete driveway last?

With proper installation, 20 to 30 years or more. Our SteelX reinforcement system extends lifespan by controlling cracking and managing soil movement.

Can you repair my existing driveway instead of replacing it?

Sometimes. If cracks are minor and structural damage is limited, repair is an option. If the slab is heaving, settled, or has widespread cracking, replacement is usually the better long-term investment.

What finish do most people choose?

Broom finish is the most popular for driveways. Stamped concrete is the most popular upgrade for homeowners who want a higher-end look.
