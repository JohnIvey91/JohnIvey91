Common Signs You May Need Foundation Repair

Foundation movement often begins with small changes that become more noticeable over time.

Common warning signs include:

  • Cracks in drywall throughout the house, especially around door frames, windows, in ceilings, in corners, and along the wall
  • Doors and windows that stick or are hard to open and close
  • Uneven or sloping floors
  • Tilting chimneys
  • New floor cracks

Not every crack means your home needs foundation repair. The key is determining whether those signs are cosmetic or the result of structural movement.

Why Do Foundation Problems Occur?

Every home settles after it's built. That's normal. Problems develop when one part of the foundation moves more than another, called differential settlement. It's what causes all the common signs and symptoms of foundation movement.

In Northwest Louisiana, several factors can contribute to this type of settlement including:

Expansive Clay Soil

Much of Northwest Louisiana is built on clay soils that expand when wet and shrink when dry. Over time, those seasonal changes can cause different areas beneath your foundation to move at different rates.

Poor Drainage

Water collecting around your foundation can soften the supporting soil, while areas that stay dry may shrink. This uneven support can lead to foundation movement.

Plumbing Leaks

A leaking water or sewer line beneath the slab can wash away or soften the supporting soil, creating localized settlement.

Improper Soil Preparation Before Construction

Homes rely on properly compacted soil for support. If the soil beneath the foundation wasn't properly prepared before construction, it can settle over time.

Tree Roots

Large trees can affect the soil beneath your home in several ways. As roots grow, they may draw moisture from the soil, causing it to shrink, and they can also grow into or around underground plumbing, leading to damaged pipes or leaks. Both of which can contribute to foundation movement over time.

Flooding & Extreme Weather

Too much or too little moisture can cause the soil beneath your home to expand and shrink. Over time, these changes can lead to uneven foundation movement.
