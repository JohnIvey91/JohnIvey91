Concrete Repair

Cracked driveways, spalling patios, sunken walkways — damaged concrete is one of the most common problems homeowners in Northwest Louisiana deal with. Our climate is hard on concrete. Heavy rain, heat, and expanding clay soil put pressure on slabs year after year. The good news is that not every damaged slab needs to be torn out and replaced. SC Steel & Concrete Professionals assesses what you have, tells you straight what it will take to fix it, and does the work correctly so the repair holds.

Common Concrete Problems We Fix

Most concrete damage in our area falls into one of these categories:

  • Cracks in driveways, patios, sidewalks, or garage slabs
  • Sunken or uneven sections caused by soil settlement or washout underneath
  • Spalling surfaces where the top layer of concrete is flaking, pitting, or breaking apart
  • Deteriorating expansion joints that are letting water and debris in
  • Edge breakage along driveways and slabs where the concrete has chipped or crumbled

If you are looking at damage and not sure whether repair or replacement makes more sense, we can help you work through that decision. We will give you an honest answer, not one based on what costs more.

Why Concrete Damage Happens Here

Louisiana's expansive clay soil is the root cause of most concrete problems in this area. When clay absorbs water it swells, lifting and shifting slabs above it. When it dries out it contracts, leaving voids under the concrete that cause sections to sink or crack under load. This cycle repeats with every rain event and every dry stretch, which means it never really stops working against your concrete.

Heavy rain also erodes the base material beneath slabs over time. Once the soil washes out, the slab loses support and starts to crack or settle. Poor drainage around a slab speeds this process up considerably.

Understanding why the damage happened is part of how we approach every repair. Patching the surface without addressing what caused it is a short-term fix that fails again.

Repair or Replace: How We Help You Decide

This is the question most homeowners have when they call us, and the answer depends on a few things: how extensive the damage is, whether the base is still sound, the age of the concrete, and what the surface is used for.

Repair Usually Makes Sense When

  • Cracks are isolated and the surrounding concrete is in good condition
  • Settlement is minor and the base can be stabilized without removing the slab
  • The concrete is relatively new and structurally sound aside from surface damage
  • The repair area is small relative to the overall slab size

Replacement Usually Makes Sense When

  • Cracking is widespread and the slab has lost structural integrity
  • The base has eroded significantly and cannot support a repaired slab
  • The concrete is old, heavily spalled, and the surface is beyond patching
  • Settlement is severe enough that lifting or stabilizing the slab is not practical

We will walk you through this decision during the site visit. If repair is viable, we will tell you. If it is not, we will tell you that too, along with what a new slab would involve.

What We Repair

Crack Repair

Not all cracks are the same. Hairline cracks from normal concrete shrinkage are different from structural cracks caused by soil movement or overloading. We evaluate the crack type, width, and pattern before deciding on the right repair method. Structural cracks that are still moving require a different approach than stable cracks that just need to be sealed against water intrusion.

Concrete Leveling and Slab Lifting

Sunken sections create trip hazards, drainage problems, and in the case of driveways, damage to vehicles over time. Where the base is still sound and the slab is otherwise in good condition, we can lift and level sunken sections without tearing out the concrete. This is faster and less disruptive than full replacement when conditions support it.

Surface Repair and Resurfacing

Spalling surfaces are both an eyesore and a maintenance problem. Exposed aggregate from a deteriorating surface collects debris, becomes slippery when wet, and gets worse over time. We repair spalled areas and, where the damage is widespread across a sound slab, resurfacing is an option that restores the surface without a full replacement.

Expansion Joint Repair

Expansion joints are designed to control where concrete cracks as it expands and contracts. When the joint material deteriorates, water gets in, the edges crack, and weeds take hold. Replacing deteriorated joint material is a straightforward repair that protects the edges of the slab and extends its life.
