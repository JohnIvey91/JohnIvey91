Why Driveways Crack (And How We Prevent It)

Driveways are exposed to a lot of stress. They carry the weight of vehicles, endure temperature fluctuations, and face the wear and tear of daily use. In Louisiana, our expansive clay soil adds another layer of challenge, as it can swell when wet and shrink when dry, putting additional stress on concrete slabs. Many driveways fail because they were poured without proper site preparation, inadequate thickness, or missing reinforcement. At SC Steel, we address these issues head-on. We start with thorough site preparation, including excavation and base compaction, to create a stable foundation for the concrete. We pour driveways with the appropriate thickness based on the expected load, and we include our SteelX reinforcement system in every slab to control cracking and add tensile strength. We also cut control joints at strategic intervals to manage where cracks occur, and we ensure proper curing to allow the concrete to reach its full strength. This comprehensive approach is why our driveways stand the test of time, even in the challenging soil conditions of Northwest Louisiana.

Why Concrete Fails in Louisiana

Concrete does not just fail on its own. It fails when the prep work is wrong, the mix is wrong, or the reinforcement is missing. In Northwest Louisiana, those mistakes get punished faster than in most markets because of our expansive clay soil.

When clay soil gets wet, it swells. When it dries out, it contracts. That movement puts stress on any slab sitting on top of it. A slab poured without proper base preparation or adequate steel reinforcement will show cracks within a season or two. We see it often on properties where a previous contractor cut corners on the work that happens before the truck ever shows up.

How We Prepare and Pour

Site Prep and Base Compaction

Good concrete starts with what is underneath it. We excavate to the right depth, remove weak or unstable soil where needed, and compact a proper base before any forms go in. This step is invisible once the job is done, but it is what determines whether your slab stays flat five years from now or starts to heave and crack.

Proper Thickness for the Load

A residential walkway and a shop floor where you park a truck do not need the same thickness. We size each slab based on what it will carry. Driveways and vehicle pads are poured thicker at the edges where load stress is highest. We do not apply a one-size approach to jobs that call for different specs.

SteelX Reinforcement — Included as Standard

Every slab we pour includes our SteelX reinforcement system. This is not an upgrade or an add-on. It is how we work. Reinforcement controls cracking, adds tensile strength, and helps the slab hold together if the soil beneath it shifts. Concrete without reinforcement is a slab waiting for problems. We do not pour slabs that way.

Control Joints in the Right Places

Concrete will crack. The goal is to control where it cracks so that cracks happen where they are least visible and least damaging. We cut control joints at planned intervals based on the slab size and shape. This is basic practice that a surprising number of crews skip or space wrong.

Proper Curing

What happens after the pour matters as much as the pour itself. Concrete that dries too fast loses strength and becomes prone to surface cracking and dusting. We use curing compounds and, when conditions call for it, cover the slab to slow the drying process and let the concrete reach its full strength. You will not drive on or use the slab until it is ready.

Finish Options

We offer several surface finishes depending on where the slab will be used and what look you want:

  • Broom finish: the most common residential option, provides texture for traction and a clean, consistent look
  • Smooth trowel finish: a flat, polished surface suited for covered patios and interior shop floors
  • Exposed aggregate: the stone in the concrete mix is revealed at the surface for a textured, decorative appearance
  • Stamped concrete: a decorative finish that can mimic the look of brick, stone, or tile

We will walk you through the options and recommend what makes the most sense for your project before work begins.

Frequently Asked Questions About Concrete Driveways

How long does a concrete driveway last?

With proper installation and maintenance, 20–30 years or more. Our SteelX reinforcement system extends lifespan by controlling cracking and managing soil movement.

Can you repair my existing driveway instead of replacing it?

Sometimes. If cracks are minor and structural damage is limited, we can seal and repair. If the slab is severely heaving, settled, or has widespread cracking, replacement is usually the better long-term investment.

What finish do most people choose?

Broom finish is the most popular for driveways—it's practical, clean-looking, and provides good traction. Stamped concrete is growing in popularity for homeowners who want a high-end look.

Do you guarantee your work?

Yes. We stand behind every driveway we pour. If something is wrong, we make it right.
