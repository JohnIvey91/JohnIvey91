CONCRETE REPAIR

Not every damaged slab needs to be torn out and replaced. SC Steel & Concrete assesses what you have, tells you straight what it will take to fix it, and does the work so the repair holds.

COMMON PROBLEMS WE FIX

  • Cracks in driveways, patios, sidewalks, or garage slabs
  • Sunken or uneven sections caused by soil settlement or washout
  • Spalling surfaces where the top layer is flaking, pitting, or breaking apart
  • Deteriorating expansion joints letting water and debris in
  • Edge breakage along driveways and slabs

WHY CONCRETE FAILS IN LOUISIANA

Northwest Louisiana's expansive clay soil is the root cause of most concrete problems here. Clay swells when wet and contracts when dry, constantly shifting the slabs above it. Heavy rain erodes base material over time, leaving voids under the concrete that cause sections to sink or crack under load. Poor drainage accelerates the whole process. We find the cause before we patch anything. A surface fix on an unresolved problem fails again.

REPAIR OR REPLACE

The answer depends on how extensive the damage is, whether the base is still sound, and what the surface is used for.

REPAIR USUALLY MAKES SENSE WHEN

  • Cracks are isolated and the surrounding concrete is in good condition
  • Settlement is minor and the base can be stabilized without removing the slab
  • The concrete is structurally sound aside from surface damage
  • The repair area is small relative to the overall slab

REPLACEMENT USUALLY MAKES SENSE WHEN

  • Cracking is widespread and the slab has lost structural integrity
  • The base has eroded significantly and cannot support a repaired slab
  • The concrete is old, heavily spalled, and the surface is beyond patching
  • Settlement is severe enough that lifting or stabilizing is not practical

We will walk you through this decision at the site visit and give you a straight answer either way.

WHAT WE REPAIR

CRACK REPAIR

Not all cracks are the same. Hairline shrinkage cracks are different from structural cracks caused by soil movement. We evaluate the crack type, width, and pattern before choosing a repair method. Cracks that are still moving require a different approach than stable cracks that just need sealing against water intrusion.

CONCRETE LEVELING AND SLAB LIFTING

Sunken sections create trip hazards and drainage problems. Where the base is still sound, we can lift and level sunken sections without tearing out the concrete — faster and less disruptive than full replacement when conditions support it.

SURFACE REPAIR AND RESURFACING

Spalling surfaces get worse over time and become slippery when wet. We repair spalled areas and, where damage is widespread across an otherwise sound slab, resurfacing restores the surface without full replacement.

EXPANSION JOINT REPAIR

When joint material deteriorates, water gets in, edges crack, and weeds take hold. Replacing deteriorated joint material is a straightforward repair that protects slab edges and extends its life.
