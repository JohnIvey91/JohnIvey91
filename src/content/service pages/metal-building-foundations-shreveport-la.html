Metal Building Foundations in Shreveport, LA

A metal building is only as good as the foundation it sits on. Whether you're putting up a shop, warehouse, barn, or commercial structure, the concrete work underneath determines how long that building performs and how safe it is to use. Why Metal Building Foundations Require Specialized Work

Not every concrete contractor understands the demands of a metal building foundation. These structures transfer load differently than wood-framed buildings, and the anchor bolt placement has to be precise. If the bolts are off by even a small margin, your building package won't line up and the entire erection process gets complicated and expensive. We work directly from your building manufacturer's drawings to set anchor bolts at the exact spacing, depth, and projection required. Every pour is inspected before the concrete sets to make sure everything lines up before it's too late to make changes.

Foundation Types for Metal Buildings

Metal buildings can be installed on different foundation types depending on building use, local codes, and soil conditions. We help you select the appropriate foundation for your situation.

  • Slab-On-Grade — Most common and economical option. Concrete slab provides both foundation and floor in one installation. Best for workshops, garages, and storage where an interior concrete floor works well. Requires adequate site prep and base material for Louisiana soils.
  • Perimeter Foundation with Gravel Floor — Concrete perimeter beam supports the building with a gravel interior floor. Economical for agricultural or storage buildings where a concrete floor isn't needed. Perimeter beam extends below frost line and provides anchor bolt attachment.
  • Pier Foundation — Concrete piers at building corners and intervals support the structure above grade. Used for buildings on slopes or where an elevated floor prevents moisture issues. Requires proper design for Louisiana wind loads and soil conditions.

Site Preparation and Soil Work

A concrete slab is only as strong as what's underneath it. Shreveport's clay-heavy soil expands and contracts with moisture, which can cause slabs to crack and shift if the base isn't prepared correctly. We grade and compact the subgrade, bring in the right amount of crushed stone base, and install a moisture barrier before any concrete is poured. If your site has drainage issues or poor soil conditions, we address those problems before the pour so they don't become your problem after the building goes up. Proper site prep is the step most contractors rush, and it's the one that matters most.

Reinforcement and Load Requirements

Metal buildings put concentrated loads at the column base plates, which means the concrete directly under those points takes the most stress. We design our reinforcement layouts to account for those load points, using rebar and wire mesh sized to your building's requirements. For heavier structures or buildings that will house forklifts, heavy equipment, or large vehicles, we increase the slab thickness and rebar schedule to handle the added weight. Everything we do on the reinforcement side is driven by your building specs and the intended use of the structure, not by what's cheapest or fastest.
