    • The cost depends on the size and scope of work, site conditions, demolition, dirt work, accessibility, concrete mix specifications (psi, thickness, additives, etc), reinforcement requirements, and current scheduling demand. Because every project is different, we provide proposals based on the specific scope of work.

    • Most flatworks like driveways, patios, and sidewalks take approximately 2 to 5 days to complete, depending on the square footage, site conditions (if there’s tree roots to remove, lots of pipes in the ground, access to site, etc), and complexity of the project.

      The process typically includes dirt work, site preparation, formwork, pouring and finishing, and final clean up.

      Larger or more complex projects may take longer, and weather conditions can also affect the schedule.

    • Yes. Concrete is extremely strong under compression, but it has low tensile strength, meaning it is less resistant to forces that pull or stretch it apart. As concrete cures, shrinks, and responds to changes in the temperature and ground movement, some cracking (including hairline cracking) can naturally occur.

      Our job is to reduce, control, and direct cracking through proper site preparation, reinforcement, concrete placement, and strategically placed keyways.

      While no concrete contractor can guarantee that concrete will never crack, proper construction methods help control where the cracking occurs and reduce the likelihood of larger, irregular, wider cracks.

    • Most residential concrete driveways are typically 4 inches thick, but the right thickness depends on how the driveway will be used, site conditions, and the types of vehicles it will support.

    • Yes. For clients who want additional strength and durability, we can customize the driveway with upgrades such as a 6-inch, higher psi concrete, additional reinforcement, or other specific options.

      Whether you need a standard driveway or want to invest in a more robust driveway, we can discuss the available options and build according to your needs, preferences, and budget.

    • We recommend that you wait a minimum of 3 days after the driveway is poured before driving on it. If possible, waiting 7 days is preferred to allow the concrete additional time to cure and gain strength.

    • Both traditional and post-tension slabs use rebar to reinforce the concrete. The main difference is that a post-tension slab also contains high-strength steel cables. After the concrete has hardened, these cables are tightened to place pressure on the concrete, making the foundation stronger and helping reduce cracking and movement.

      Both types of slabs can be strong and long-lasting when properly built. Post-tension slabs are often used when the structure, soil conditions, or engineered design require additional strength and greater control of cracking and movement.

      It is important to know if your home has a post-tension slab before remodeling, cutting, drilling, or sawing into the concrete. Steel & Concrete Pros installs both traditional and post-tension slabs and repairs damaged or snapped post-tension cables.

    • No. Steel & Concrete Pros is the contractor that performs the foundation repair work. A licensed professional engineer is responsible for performing the final engineering inspection and issuing any engineer-stamped letter or documentation.

      We strongly recommend having the completed repairs inspected by a licensed professional engineer. If cracks, previous foundation movement, or signs of structural repairs are discovered during a future home inspection, sale, or refinancing, you may be asked to provide documentation showing that the foundation repairs were completed and evaluated by a licensed professional engineer.

      Steel & Concrete Pros can provide photographs and documentation of the repair work we perform, but contractor records are not substitutes for a licensed engineer stamped letter.

      Clients may hire and coordinate directly with the licensed engineer, which is typically the most cost-effective option. Steel & Concrete Pros can also coordinate the final engineering inspection as an additional service.

      Simply put: we perform the foundation repairs. The licensed professional engineer performs the final inspection and issues the engineer-stamped documentation.

      We strongly recommend completing both parts of the process so you have the appropriate documentation for your records and for any future sale or refinancing of the property.
