    PATIOS & WALKWAYS

    A patio is more than just a place to relax and entertain. It's the heart of your outdoor living space, where memories are made and family gatherings take place. But a cracked, uneven, or settling patio doesn't just look bad—it's a safety hazard and a sign of poor installation. In Northwest Louisiana, the challenge is real: our expansive clay soil swells when wet and shrinks when dry, putting constant stress on concrete slabs. Most patios fail within 5–10 years because contractors skip proper site prep, use inadequate thickness, or omit reinforcement entirely. SC Steel & Concrete Professionals builds patios engineered to last 20+ years, even under Louisiana's toughest soil conditions.

    Why Patios Crack (And How We Prevent It)

    Patios are exposed to a lot of stress. They carry the weight of furniture and people, endure temperature fluctuations, and face the wear and tear of daily use. In Louisiana, our expansive clay soil adds another layer of challenge, as it can swell when wet and shrink when dry, putting additional stress on concrete slabs. Many patios fail because they were poured without proper site preparation, inadequate thickness, or missing reinforcement. At SC Steel, we address these issues head-on. We start with thorough site preparation, including excavation and base compaction, to create a stable foundation for the concrete. We pour patios with the appropriate thickness based on the expected load, and we include our SteelX reinforcement system in every slab to control cracking and add tensile strength. We also cut control joints at strategic intervals to manage where cracks occur, and we ensure proper curing to allow the concrete to reach its full strength. This comprehensive approach is why our patios stand the test of time, even in the challenging soil conditions of Northwest Louisiana.

    Why Concrete Fails in Louisiana

    Concrete does not just fail on its own. It fails when the prep work is wrong, the mix is wrong, or the reinforcement is missing. In Northwest Louisiana, those mistakes get punished faster than in most markets because of our expansive clay soil.

    When clay soil gets wet, it swells. When it dries out, it contracts. That movement puts stress on any slab sitting on top of it. A slab poured without proper base preparation or adequate steel reinforcement will show cracks within a season or two. We see it often on properties where a previous contractor cut corners on the work that happens before the truck ever shows up.

    How We Prepare and Pour

    Site Prep and Base Compaction

    Good concrete starts with what is underneath it. We excavate to the right depth, remove weak or unstable soil where needed, and compact a proper base before any forms go in. This step is invisible once the job is done, but it is what determines whether your slab stays flat five years from now or starts to heave and crack.

    Proper Thickness for the Load

    A residential walkway and a shop floor where you park a truck do not need the same thickness. We size each slab based on what it will carry. Driveways and vehicle pads are poured thicker at the edges where load stress is highest. We do not apply a one-size approach to jobs that call for different specs.

    SteelX Reinforcement — Included as Standard

    Every slab we pour includes our SteelX reinforcement system. This is not an upgrade or an add-on. It is how we work. Reinforcement controls cracking, adds tensile strength, and helps the slab hold together if the soil beneath it shifts. Concrete without reinforcement is a slab waiting for problems. We do not pour slabs that way.

    Control Joints in the Right Places

    Concrete will crack. The goal is to control where it cracks so that cracks happen where they are least visible and least damaging. We cut control joints at planned intervals based on the slab size and shape. This is basic practice that a surprising number of crews skip or space wrong.

    Proper Curing

    What happens after the pour matters as much as the pour itself. Concrete that dries too fast loses strength and becomes prone to surface cracking and dusting. We use curing compounds and, when conditions call for it, cover the slab to slow the drying process and let the concrete reach its full strength. You will not drive on or use the slab until it is ready.

    Finish Options

    We offer several surface finishes depending on where the slab will be used and what look you want:

    • Broom finish: the most common residential option, provides texture for traction and a clean, consistent look
    • Smooth trowel finish: a flat, polished surface suited for covered patios and interior shop floors
    • Exposed aggregate: the stone in the concrete mix is revealed at the surface for a textured, decorative appearance
    • Stamped concrete: a decorative finish that can mimic the look of brick, stone, or tile

    We will walk you through the options and recommend what makes the most sense for your project before work begins.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Concrete Patios

    What's the best finish for a patio?

    Broom finish is popular and practical for most patios—it provides traction and a clean look. If you want something more decorative, stamped concrete mimics brick or stone and really stands out. We'll help you choose based on your budget and style.

    Can I add a roof or cover to my patio later?

    Absolutely. Many of our customers pour a patio and add a covered structure later. We'll size and reinforce the slab appropriately if you think you might want this option down the road.

    How do I maintain my concrete patio?

    Sweep off debris, seal the surface every 2–3 years, and clean with mild soap and water. Avoid harsh chemicals and power washers on high pressure. Proper sealing extends the life significantly and keeps it looking new.

    Can you pour a patio around existing landscaping?

    Yes. We work around trees, shrubs, and landscape features. We can even custom-form around them to maintain your existing design.

    What if my patio already has problems?

    Sometimes we can repair and seal existing patios. If the damage is severe (major cracking, settling, heaving), replacement is usually the better long-term solution. We'll evaluate and give you honest options.
