    Warning Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

    Foundation problems rarely fix themselves. What starts as a small crack in your drywall or a door that sticks can turn into a serious structural issue if left alone. Shreveport's clay-heavy soil expands and contracts with moisture, putting constant pressure on foundation walls and footings.

    The earlier you catch a problem, the less the repair costs. Watch for:

    • Cracks in drywall, especially above doorways or running diagonally from corners
    • Stair-step cracks in exterior brick or block
    • Doors or windows that stick, drag, or no longer latch
    • Gaps forming between walls and the ceiling or floor
    • Floors that feel uneven, soft in spots, or slightly sloped
    • New cracks in tile, along slab edges, or in your patio

    None of these fix themselves. A quick inspection now can prevent a much larger repair bill later.

    How We Stabilize and Repair Your Foundation

    Every foundation repair starts with understanding what caused the problem. We inspect from the inside and outside, check for drainage issues, and assess the soil conditions around your home. From there, we recommend the right repair method for your situation.

    Diagnose Before We Quote

    Before we recommend anything, we understand exactly what is driving the damage. A surface fix on a deeper problem just fails again. We find the cause and fix that.

    Pier and Underpinning Systems

    When a foundation has settled or shifted, piers are one of the most reliable ways to stabilize it. Steel push piers are driven deep into stable soil or bedrock below the frost line, transferring the weight of your home away from the compromised soil near the surface.

    Helical piers work the same way but are screwed into the ground and are a strong option for lighter loads or areas with limited access. Both systems are engineered to hold your home in place long after the repair is done. We pull permits, follow local codes, and document everything so you have a record if you ever sell your home.

    Bowed Walls and Lateral Pressure

    A bowed or leaning foundation wall is a sign that soil pressure from outside is pushing against your home. Left alone, bowed walls can crack, buckle, and eventually fail. We use wall anchors and carbon fiber straps to stop the movement and reinforce the wall from the inside.

    Wall anchors connect your foundation wall to stable soil further out in your yard and can be tightened over time to gradually straighten the wall. Carbon fiber straps bond directly to the surface of the wall and prevent further inward movement without taking up space inside your basement or crawl space.

    Post-Tension Slab Specialists

    Many homes in Shreveport and Bossier City have post-tension slab foundations. Cutting or nicking a post-tension cable during a repair can compromise the entire slab. Our team is trained to work on these systems safely and correctly. Most local contractors are not.
